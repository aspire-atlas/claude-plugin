# Glossary and banned phrases

The words every page uses for the same thing, and the phrases no page uses. The page check reads
the banned-phrase list below (R11) and so does the fill script, so a page made either way is held
to the same list. The rubric (`rubric.md`) says how to fix a page that uses one.

## Contents

- [1. Glossary](#1-glossary)
- [2. Banned phrases](#2-banned-phrases)

## 1. Glossary

Say each measure the way it is written here, every time, and define it on the page where the
reader first meets it when the reader may not know it.

| Term | What it means on a page |
| ---- | ----------------------- |
| Views | How many times a post was played or shown, as the network counts it. An Instagram image has none |
| Engagement per 1,000 views | Likes, comments, shares and saves for every 1,000 views (`SKILL.md`, section 8). A page that counts fewer parts names them and never uses this name |
| Median | The middle value of the posts counted: half did better, half did worse. Pages compare with the median, never the average, unless they say so |
| The usual | An account's own median over the window the page names ("3.1 times her usual") |
| Baseline | The figure a page compares with: the usual, the prior period, peers of the same size, or a goal. Every number on a page has one |
| Directional | A figure from too few posts to rely on, marked as `comparisons.md` says |
| Share of voice | A brand's share of the posts, or of the views, about the brands compared, over the same window |
| Hook rate | The share of people who watch past the first 3 seconds of a video |
| Paid label, no paid label | Whether a post by someone other than the brand carries the network's paid partnership label (`post-kinds.md`). Never "organic" or "unpaid" |
| Insight | A finding that is surprising, explained, and matters to the reader, with its evidence, its comparison and what to do about it. A page has at most three |
| Next step | An action with a verb, an object, an owner, a date, the effect it should have and its cost |
| Decision | What the reader must approve, kept apart from the next steps (R6) |
| Source label | Where a claim came from: Atlas, Brand memory, Web, You, Our read, or a connected tool by the name the reader knows (`templates.md`, section 2) |
| Confidence | How sure the page is (high, medium or low) and why, in one line |
| Freshness | How old the figures are: the newest post Atlas holds and when it was last fetched (`SKILL.md`, section 9) |

## 2. Banned phrases

No page uses these, outside quoted words (a creator's caption, someone's own words). They are
filler, sales talk or vague verbs: each line gives the forms the check refuses and what to say
instead. Agent, tool and system names are refused too; that list is the page check's (R11 in
`reader-standard.md`).

Each line is the forms, separated by commas, then `|` and what to write instead. The check matches
each form as whole words, in any case. To add a phrase, add a line here; nothing else changes.
