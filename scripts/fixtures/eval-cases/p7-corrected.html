Six concepts, twelve creators, thirty ads from one shoot each: Inkwell's creator ad campaign

For the client's budget owner | Inkwell, an essay checker for college students with a writing-replay browser extension | 6 concepts (3 student voice, 3 teacher voice), 12 creator options, 30 variants

The pitch

Six concepts, each with one job and two creator options to choose from. Every creator films one shoot that we cut into five ads, so results compare cleanly across creators.

The market problem

"Nearly 3 in 4 college students are at least moderately worried about being wrongly accused of using AI." Source: student survey, about 700 students, January 2026.

"68% of middle and high school teachers use AI detection tools." Source: national teacher survey, 2023–24 school year.

Strategy

One promise, from Inkwell's FAQ: "Check your essay before your teacher does." (Inkwell's FAQ page)

  1. Every ad ends on a student.
  2. The screen is the proof.
  3. Sell the check; never promise no flag.

One shoot per creator

Shot list: Hooks A, B and C; 1 body; CTAs 1 and 2; b-roll 01 to 15. That is 21 assets, cut into 5 ads.

The variant recipe

Why this scheme for Inkwell: your top posts all turn on the scan reveal, so every variant keeps the product's own screen in shot, and V4 leads with it. Students buy after a free scan, so V5 is a short direct-response cut to the free tier. The same five variants for every creator, so results compare cleanly across creators.

Claims

Your team signs off on both lists before briefing.

Will say

  • Check your essay before you submit it. (Inkwell home page)
  • See which parts might read as AI. (Inkwell features page)
  • Keep a record of how you wrote it. (Inkwell extension page)
  • Free to start, 10,000 words a month. (Inkwell pricing page)

Won't say or show

  • "You'll never be flagged."
  • Accuracy percentages.
  • Anything that helps disguise AI-written text.
  • A real school, teacher or student.
  • Real student essays on screen.
